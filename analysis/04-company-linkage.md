# 4. Company Linkage — Core & Main (CNM) and Ferguson (FERG)

*All company financials sourced from Quartr (filings, earnings decks, call transcripts).*

## 4.1 Two very different ways to own the same theme

| | **Core & Main** | **Ferguson Enterprises** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ticker | CNM (NYSE) | FERG (NYSE) |
| Latest FY revenue | **$7,647m** (FY ended 1 Feb 2026, +2.8%) | **$31,316m** (calendar 2025, +5.0%) |
| Latest quarter | Q1 FY26 (to 3 May 2026): $1,911m, **flat** | Q2 CY26 (to 30 Jun 2026): $8,751m, **+4.6%** |
| Profitability | Adj. EBITDA $931m, **12.2%** margin (FY25) | Adj. op. profit $932m, **10.7%** margin (Q2 CY26); adj. EBITDA $994m |
| Water exposure | **100% of revenue** is water/wastewater/storm/fire | **Waterworks = 24% of US net sales** (Q2 CY26) |
| Estimated waterworks revenue | **$7.6bn** (all of it) | **~$6.5–7.1bn** (see note on seasonality) |
| Municipal exposure | **44% of net sales** ≈ $3.37bn | Not separately disclosed; waterworks spans municipal, public works, treatment and residential development |
| Repair & replace vs new build | **50 / 50** | Not disclosed |
| Stated market position | **20% share of a $39bn US TAM**; 17% of a $44bn US+Canada TAM | Largest US distributor overall; TAM rises to **$400bn** post-FloWorks |
| Scale | 370+ branches, ~5,600 associates, 225k+ SKUs | 1,500+ branches; next-day delivery to 95% of pro customers |
| Guidance | FY26: **$7.8–7.9bn** net sales (+2–3%), adj. EBITDA $950–980m | CY26: **mid-single-digit** sales growth (raised); op. margin 9.5–9.8% |

**Note on Ferguson's waterworks figure.** The 24% share is disclosed for the quarter ended
30 June 2026 — seasonally the strongest waterworks quarter. Applying it to full-year US net
sales of roughly $29.7bn (calendar 2025 group revenue of $31,316m less an estimated ~$1.6bn
Canada, annualised from Q2's $408m) gives ~$7.1bn; a 22% full-year share gives ~$6.5bn.
Ferguson does not disclose a full-year waterworks percentage, so treat this as an estimate,
not a reported figure.

### The combined market position

CNM's $7.65bn plus Ferguson's estimated $6.5–7.1bn of waterworks revenue is
**$14.2–14.8bn against the $39bn US TAM Core & Main cites — a combined ~36–38% share** of a
market both describe as fragmented. That is a duopoly at the top of a long tail, and it is
the structural reason both have been able to consolidate (130+ M&A deals between them over
the past decade) and hold pricing.

Sanity check on CNM's own disclosure: 20% of $39bn = $7.8bn versus reported net sales of
$7.65bn. Internally consistent, which raises confidence in the TAM figure.

## 4.2 Sensitivity: what a change in national water spending is actually worth

Using the model in [§2.5](02-forward-budget.md#25-build-up-model-national-water-capital-spending-20272035):

| Change | Core & Main | Ferguson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **+100bp** to municipal market growth | +$34m revenue = **+0.44% of total net sales**; ~+$4m adj. EBITDA (**+0.4%**) | +$71m revenue = **+0.23% of group revenue** |
| Municipal / waterworks as % of group revenue | **44%** | **~23%** |
| Federal IIJA drag at peak (~1.0–1.5pt of market growth, 2029–30) | ~45–70bps of total revenue growth | ~25–35bps of group revenue growth |

**The conclusion investors most often get wrong: the IIJA cliff is not a thesis-breaking
event for either company.** Even at full effect, and even for the pure-play, it is worth
well under a percentage point of revenue growth. The residential end market — 18% of CNM's
mix, ~50% of Ferguson's US mix — moves both companies far more.

## 4.3 What management actually says about the funding cliff

Core & Main was asked directly on the 10 June 2026 call. The answer is worth reading in full
because it is the most specific public account of where the IIJA money physically sits.

**Brad Cowles, President:**

> "On IIJA, the remaining funding is expected to hit the state revolving funds this year.
> That doesn't mean that there's any cliff to the funding or there's any end to the funding.
> A lot of the funding has already hit the state revolving funds. **Only about a third or
> less of it has hit the municipality level yet.** … A portion of that funding is in the
> grant form, and then the other portion is in low interest loans. **A portion of that would
> get repaid back into those state revolving funds and used for future sources.**"

**Robyn Bradbury, CFO:**

> "95% of the funding that municipalities use for their water infrastructure is state and
> local, and we think that those are really strong. We're seeing municipalities increase
> water rates to their customers to be able to afford some of the upgrades. We're seeing
> municipal bond growth and the municipal bond issuance that's going out there."

Both claims check out against independent data — CBO puts the state/local share at 92–96%,
and Bluefield measures 2025 rate growth at +5.1%. This is the rare case where management
commentary and third-party data agree, which materially raises the confidence weighting.

**On treatment plants** (Brad Cowles, same call), the durability argument:

> "This is just a sort of a non-discretionary investment that the municipalities they must
> do… We see it as a pretty durable long-term trend that is driving good growth for us."

Ferguson's framing of the same demand pool (Kevin Murphy, CEO, 10 August 2026):

> "We do see good, broad-based, large capital construction project growth. Data center
> construction activity continues to be the strength of that sector, but we're also seeing
> good growth… in areas like power generation, in areas like chemical, food and beverage,
> general manufacturing, mining and minerals, **obviously water and wastewater treatment**."

## 4.4 Where the growth is actually coming from

Neither company is growing at the market rate. Both are growing *within* it by moving into
higher-value, more technical categories — which is the same conclusion the national model
reached in [§2.7](02-forward-budget.md#27-what-this-means-for-the-distributors).

### Core & Main's two identified above-market lines

| Initiative | Five-year sales CAGR | Size | Driver |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Treatment plant solutions** | 15–25% | mid-single-digit % of net sales | Aging facilities + regulatory requirements; "supported by durable funding" |
| **Smart utility / metering (AMI)** | 15–25% | Not disclosed; growing double digits | Multi-year municipal AMI programmes; non-revenue water economics |

Management is explicit that these are *capability* plays, not distribution plays — engineering,
estimating and project management added to win "large, multi-year treatment plant builds",
and turnkey AMI including software, analytics, installation and service. The M&A pipeline is
being pointed at the same target: *"how can we continue to bring more of that knowledge,
expertise, talent, and capability."* CEO Mark Witkowski also flagged a "notable uptick" in the
M&A pipeline after an extended lull — worth monitoring, since CNM's history is
acquisition-led.

Also notable: CNM added **Susan Hardwick, former CEO of American Water**, to its board — a
direct signal of intent around the municipal and regulated-private-water customer.

### Ferguson's positioning

Ferguson is playing the **large capital project** theme across customer groups rather than
water specifically. Large capital projects are "mid- to high-single-digit percentage of total
Ferguson revenue, and we continue to trend up within that range," with backlogs "continuing to
be above what those growth rates were for the quarter" — **including waterworks**, which had a
"lumpier" Q2 at +3% against a +15% comparable.

The **FloWorks acquisition** ($1.6bn, ~$1bn revenue, ~10x LTM adj. EBITDA including synergies,
expected close Q3 2026) is explicitly justified on cross-selling into "waterworks, commercial,
mechanical, and industrial" for data centres, chip production, power generation and water.

## 4.5 The commodity and pricing overlay

A distinct variable that is easy to conflate with volume:

| | Core & Main (Q1 FY26) | Ferguson (Q2 CY26) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pricing | Roughly **flat**; "virtually every product category was either flat or up. **PVC was a headwind**" | Inflation decelerated from mid- to **upper-low-single-digit**; commodities ~15% of revenue |
| PVC specifically | Supplier price increase announcements received, **not yet in revenue** | "PVC is still very much in deflation… **down about in the double-digit range for the quarter**" |
| Gross margin | 27.2%, **+50bps** — private label, sourcing, disciplined pricing | 31.0%, −20bps; company sees 30–31% as the range |
| Tariffs | Cited as a product-cost risk factor | >90% of purchases from branded manufacturers; tariff refunds "modest, nothing material" |

**PVC deflation is currently masking underlying municipal volume strength at both companies.**
Core & Main delivered flat revenue with +50bps of gross margin and +6% adjusted EPS; that is a
volume-and-mix story running underneath a price headwind. If announced resin-driven PVC
increases stick, reported revenue growth would accelerate without any change in underlying
demand — and the reverse is the risk if they do not.

## 4.6 What to monitor, ranked by signal value

1. **Water and sewer rate increases** (Bluefield rate index, individual utility rate cases).
   This is 95% of the funding. It ran +5.1% in 2025 and +24.2% over five years. A deceleration
   here matters more than anything Congress does.
2. **AWWA v. EPA** — oral argument expected Fall 2026. The largest binary swing factor in the
   forecast. An adverse ruling on utility-controlled line replacement removes a multi-billion
   dollar annual programme.
3. **WRDA 2026 → appropriations.** The Senate EPW bill would authorise $14bn/4yr CWSRF and
   $16.5bn/5yr DWSRF. Watch whether authorisation converts to appropriation; the FY2027 House
   bill currently proposes a **16% SRF cut**.
4. **Census C30 construction put in place**, water supply + sewage — monthly, leads distributor
   revenue by two to four quarters.
5. **Municipal bond issuance for water and sewer.** The capital vehicle. Issuers deferred
   through the 2023–24 rate spike and have been returning.
6. **PVC resin pricing.** Determines whether reported revenue growth reflects volume or price.
7. **Residential construction.** 18% of CNM, ~50% of Ferguson's US mix — currently the actual
   drag on both, and far larger than the federal funding question.
8. **Data centre water infrastructure announcements** in Virginia, Texas, Arizona, Louisiana,
   Indiana — the genuinely new demand pool, though politically contested.

## 4.7 Synthesis

- **The market is mid-single-digit nominal growth, replacement-led, low-cyclicality and
  regulation-anchored.** It is not a boom, and it will not be one.
- **Core & Main is the higher-beta, pure-play expression.** 44% municipal, 100% water,
  50% repair-and-replace, and a stated 20% share of a $39bn TAM. It has the most leverage to
  the municipal cycle and the most exposure to residential weakness at 18% of mix. Its FY26
  guidance of +2–3% is *below* the market growth rate the national model implies, which sets
  a low bar if municipal strength persists and PVC pricing normalises.
- **Ferguson is the diversified expression.** Waterworks is ~23% of group revenue and its
  growth decelerated to +3% on a +15% comparable. The water thesis is real but diluted;
  the offsetting driver — data centres and large capital projects across commercial/mechanical
  and industrial — is currently stronger, which is a feature if you want the infrastructure
  theme without municipal-budget risk, and a bug if you want a clean water proxy.
- **Both are consolidators in a fragmented tail.** With ~36–38% combined share, the durable
  earnings driver is M&A and category expansion into treatment and metering — not the
  underlying market's growth rate.
- **The IIJA cliff is the most over-discussed risk in the sector.** It is worth well under
  100bps of revenue growth to either company, concentrated in 2029–30, against a funding base
  that is 92–96% state and local and growing at 5%+ through rate increases.
